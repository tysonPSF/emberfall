# Emberfall

Classic-EverQuest-style RPG in Godot 4.7 (GDScript). Solo slice now; multiplayer/grouping planned.

## Architecture rules

- **All state changes go through the `World` autoload** (`scripts/autoload/world.gd`). Input and UI call `World.request_*(entity_id, ...)` with ids, never mutate Entity stats/inventory directly. These functions become server RPCs later; keep them id-based and self-validating (range, ownership, dead checks).
- Networking is the `Net` autoload (`scripts/autoload/net.gd`): a dedicated server (`--server`) runs World; clients mirror entities (puppets under the server's ids) and forward every `request_*`. Every new `request_*` must start with `if _remote(&"request_x", [args...]): return`, and HUD signals for one player go through `World._ui(p, &"signal", [args])`, never `signal.emit()`. Rules run only where `Net.is_authority()`; mobs/npcs/corpses on a client must not simulate. A server-side change to a player's position must call `Net.teleport(p, pos)`. A server runs every visited zone at once, each in its own SubViewport world with overlapping coordinates: find an entity's zone with `World.zone_of(node)` (never `World.zone`, which is only the client's/offline current zone), and compare positions only through `Entity.distance_to`, which is infinite across zones. Groups live in `World.groups` (server); a player's `group` array is the view their HUD draws, pushed with their state. Mob kills credit the top damage dealer's group (`_kill_credit`, `_award_group`); spells with `"target": "group"` land on every member in range. Online characters live on the server (`AccountStore`, `scripts/server/account_store.gd`, under `--data`); clients never send saves, only the one-time import of an offline character.
- Player-facing messages go through `World.say(entity, text, color)`, not `log_message.emit`, so they can be routed per-connection later.
- Content is data: add mobs/items/spells/zones in `data/*.json`, not code. JSON numbers parse as float, so cast with `int()`.
- Nodes are built in code (no hand-authored scenes besides `scenes/main.tscn`). `Entity.make_visual(look)` builds either a rigged model or placeholder primitives.
- Art is KayKit (CC0) in `assets/`, referenced by id through `data/models.json`. All characters share the `Rig_Medium` skeleton, so one animation library (`CharacterModel.library()`) drives every model and weapons attach to bone `handslot.r` (shields to `handslot.l`). `models.json` `"grips"` turns and offsets a held model and can move it to another bone; with `"orient"` it rides one bone (`pos` on it, `out` in the orient bone's frame) but keeps another's orientation, which is how shields work: at the forearm, oriented by the chest, so they hang upright at your side whatever twist the walk puts in the arm. Try values with `--lineup=knight --action=walk --offhand=shield_round --grip=x,y,z,px,py,pz,bone,orient,ox,oy,oz` (the `behind_left` view is the gameplay angle). Poses the free packs lack (sitting on the ground) are built by `tools/blender/anims.py` on the same rig into `assets/animations/Rig_Medium_Extra.glb`, listed last in `models.json` `"animations"`; its `.import` keeps immutable tracks, or bones a clip holds still would keep the previous clip's pose. Use the glTF files; the fbx/obj folders carry `.gdignore`. Classes, mobs and items pick art with `model` / `weapon` fields.
- Creatures KayKit lacks (rat, fire beetle) are generated by `tools/blender/creatures.py`, which models, rigs and animates them from primitives and exports to `assets/creatures/`. They use `"rig": "own"` in `models.json` and carry their own clips named after actions (`idle`, `walk`, `run`, `attack`, `hit`, `death`); `CharacterModel` maps actions to KayKit clip names for everything else. It also builds bolt-on parts (gnoll head, tail): a `models.json` entry can reskin a KayKit body with `hide` (mesh parts), `tint` (multiplies a part's texture) and `attach` (scenes pinned to bones, authored in KayKit mesh space). Rebuild with `/Applications/Blender.app/Contents/MacOS/Blender -b --factory-startup -P tools/blender/creatures.py -- --out assets/creatures [--preview <dir>]`.
- Worn gear looks EverQuest-style first: `models.json` `"body_parts"` maps a wearable to another KayKit model's parts (`Knight_LegLeft`, `Rogue_Body`, `Skeleton_Rogue_Hood`...) with an optional `tint`; `CharacterModel.set_worn` copies them onto the wearer's skeleton (skins bound by bone name, rigid hats on their bone) and hides the wearer's own parts listed in `covers`. KayKit arms end in hands and legs in feet, so gloves and boots only swap arms/legs when no sleeves/pants do. A model's `"part_swaps"` limits which slots swap (skeletons: no arms, or they'd grow flesh hands); anything else falls back to the rigid gear.py pieces below.
- Worn gear is modeled in `tools/blender/gear.py` (KayKit character mesh space, Dungeon palette via props.py's Prop helpers) as rigid pieces pinned to bones: headgear on `head`, tunics on `chest`, belts on `spine`, gloves/sleeves/boots/leg plates on the `.l` bones (built left, exported mirrored as `_r`). `models.json` `"gear"` lists each wearable's pieces (regenerate with `--manifest <file>`); an item's `"wear"` field picks the wearable. Players hide their class model's own hat (`"headgear_parts"`) and show exactly what they wear; mobs show what they spawned with in their model's `"wearable_slots"`. `look.worn` carries it to other players. Rebuild with `/Applications/Blender.app/Contents/MacOS/Blender -b --factory-startup -P tools/blender/gear.py -- --out assets/gear [--preview <dir>]` (the preview dresses every class in cloth, leather and iron outfits).
- World props live in `models.json` `"props"` and are placed by `Zone._prop(id, pos, yaw, scale, collide)`. KayKit Dungeon pieces (walls, floors, barrels, banners) use `"scale": 0.75` to match our 0.75-scale characters. Props the pack lacks (trees, boulders, tent, campfire, obelisk, palisade, torch post, banner pole) come from `tools/blender/props.py`, in game meters, UV-mapped onto the pack's palette texture (`dungeon_texture.png`, an 8x4 grid of vertical gradients) so they match its colors and shading. New props should use the same `Prop` helpers and swatches. Buildings should match the KayKit houses: build their stone out of the pack's own pieces with `kaykit(piece, loc, rot_z, scale)` and `join_into` (the tavern's walls, windows, pillars), and give everything hand-modeled `Prop.build(bevel=0.06)` so edges are rounded like KayKit's.
- **Before creating or connecting a zone, read `docs/zone-implementation.md`.** The world's shape is settled: 36 zones in 6 areas, levels 1-50, laid out in `docs/world-layout.json` (`docs/world-map.png` is the picture) and verified by `tools/world_layout.py`. A border is FOUR things that must agree - a `passes` entry and a `zone_lines` entry on each side - so derive all four from the layout's `links` rather than authoring them per zone; that brief gives the arithmetic, measured off Emberhold, Greenmoor and Thornwood. Do not add a zone, move a cell or invent a border outside `tools/world_layout.py`, and do not give The Grove a pass, a zone line or a cell: it is teleport-only and its isolation is asserted.
- Zones are `data/zones/<id>.json`. Besides terrain, trees and spawns they hold `landmarks` (typed builders in `zone.gd`: obelisk, camp, ruins, outpost, wall_ring, hearth_plaza, house, market, signpost, cave, prop), `roads` (dirt polylines, optional `road_lamps` spacing), `passes` (gaps in the mountain ring), `zone_lines` (trigger box -> `to` zone + `arrive` point), `npcs`, and `clutter` (grass/flowers/bushes per m², drawn as chunked MultiMesh with `scripts/world/clutter.gdshader`; grass is modeled white and tinted to the ground). `main.gd` owns zone switching (`World.zone_change`); player corpses are saved per zone. New characters start in `config.starting_zone` (Emberhold); the autotest starts in Greenmoor.
- **Before creating or connecting any zone, read `docs/zone-implementation.md`.** The world is a grid of 36 zones in `docs/world-layout.json` (human version `docs/world-layout.md`, picture `docs/world-map.png`), the source of truth for where each zone is, its size and levels, and who it borders. Every border (a pass and a zone line on both sides, and the arrive points) is derived from the layout's `links`, never typed by hand. `python3 tools/world_layout.py` re-checks the layout and rewrites `docs/world-layout.json` (`python3 tools/render_map.py` redraws `docs/world-map.png` from it; needs matplotlib); do not add zones, move cells or invent borders outside it. Interiors (the tavern) and dungeon instances are off the grid: they are reached through a door or zone line inside a zone, not a pass.
- Zones so far: Emberhold (city) - Greenmoor (levels 1-7; south pass to Emberhold, north pass to Thornwood) - Thornwood Vale (`thornwood`, 512 m, levels 6-14): Elowen the Hermit at the obelisk (two quests, sells ammo), wolves and spiderlings at the south edge, bears and thornback spiders west (Broodmother Silkfang's nest at -165, -104), the Bloodtusk orc camp east (Warchief Grolthar, faction `bloodtusk`), skeleton knights at the northern watchtower ruins (Captain Veyl). Its north, east and west passes are `"rockslide"` landmarks: a future zone replaces one with a zone line. Zone data can set `tree_mix`, `groves` (0-1, trees packed into woods), `grass_colors`, `fog_density`, `fog_color` and `sun_energy`. `rivers` ([{points (downstream), width, depth, bank, flow}]) carve a wadeable channel whose water level only falls downstream, draw a flowing water ribbon (water.gdshader `flow_speed` runs ripples along UV.y) and keep trees and clutter off it; a `"bridge"` landmark on a road crossing sets the `bridge_wood` prop at bank height. Rivers must end inside the mountain ring. More landmark types: `watchtower` (KayKit walls, three storeys, ruined), `spider_nest` (cobweb, web_mound, egg_sacs props), `cabin` (house + woodpile, drying_rack, campfire). A mountain pass only opens its own edge.
- Monsters can have a natural `"proc"` ({spell, chance, text}) like weapons do (spider venom, shaman bolts); monster-only spells have empty `classes`. Dots may set `dot_text` / `land_text`.
- Greenmoor's dungeon entrance is a `"cave"` landmark at (-150, -30), mouth facing east. Behind it goes Nick's quest dungeon, which will be an **instance** (its own copy per group); the server has no instancing yet (`main.gd` runs one copy of each zone id in `_server_zones`), so that is work to do with the dungeon. The landmark is the `cave_entrance` prop from props.py, torches and boulders. Its tunnel is walkable 9-11 m back to a dark wall at about x = -159; a dungeon hooks up with a zone line there, e.g. `{"pos": [-157.5, -30], "size": [3, 4], "to": "<dungeon>", "arrive": [...]}` in greenmoor.json.
- Faction lives in `data/factions.json` (defaults, `on_kill` / `on_attack` hits, mob `kos_at`); NPCs name theirs in `data/npcs.json`. Standing rules are in `World` (`standing`, `apply_faction`, `refuses`, `mob_kos`, `npc_kos`). Players may fight an NPC only after confirming (double Q) - `World.can_attack` is the single gate for who may hit whom. Guards (`"guard"` in npcs.json) can walk a `"patrol"` of waypoints given in the zone's npc entry, help only players at `assist_standing` or better, and attack any monster inside `hunt_radius`.
- Items live in `data/items/*.json` (weapons, armor, jewelry, drops, rewards); every file in the folder is merged, so add a new file for a new quest line instead of editing a shared one. Fields beyond stats: `lore` (one per person), `no_drop`, `classes`, `deities`, `rec_level` (weaker below it), `stack`, attributes (`str sta agi wis int haste hp_regen mana_regen`), `proc` ({spell, chance} on hit) and `click` ({spell, recast}, right-click when equipped). Slots: primary, secondary (shields, shown in the left hand), head, neck, arms, hands, chest, waist, legs, feet, ring (fits ring1/ring2).
- Skills are EQ-style (`data/skills.json`): per-class caps of per_level x (level + 1), raised by use via `World.try_skill_up` (never from gray targets). Weapons name their `"skill"`; spells name their school or ability skill. Effects are measured against 80% of cap (`tuning.neutral`), where combat is exactly the base rules: weapon + offense adjust hit and damage, defense lowers the enemy's hit, block/parry/dodge avoid swings, school skill sets fizzles, channeling resists interruption from hits, meditate scales resting mana, and kick/bash/bind wound/taunt scale with their skill.
- Inventory is EQ-style: `Pack` (`scripts/core/pack.gd`) holds 8 general slots of entries `{item, count[, contents]}`; bags (`"bag": n` items in `data/items/containers.json`) hold entries but never other bags. Places are strings: `g:3`, `b:3:5` (bag in general 3, slot 5), `e:head`, `k:7` (bank), `t:2` (trade). Items move through the server-side cursor: `World.request_click(player, place)` picks up or puts down/swaps, `request_pick_one` splits a stack; shift-click shortcuts (`request_equip`, `request_sell`, `request_bank_deposit`, `request_trade_add`) take a place too. Never touch a flat item list: use `p.pack` (`add`, `remove`, `count`, `room_for`, `item_ids`) and `p.owned_item_ids()`. Giving: a click on an npc with an item on the cursor calls `World.request_give` (opens a trade, even with a merchant, and puts the item in it); G on a merchant opens a trade instead of the shop when `_hand_in_waiting` (you carry everything for one of their quests). Dropping: with an item on the cursor, a click on the world calls `World.request_drop` (NO DROP, or a bag holding one, is refused), which leaves a `GroundItem` (`scripts/entities/ground_item.gd`, the whole entry, bags with contents) in the zone for 15 minutes; clicking it calls `request_pickup` (cursor first, else the pack; lore checked). Ground items replicate like corpses (`"kind": "ground"`) and aren't saved. Icons are rendered by `tools/blender/icons.py` into `assets/icons/<item>.png` (`GameData.item_icon`).
- A spell can `"requires": "shield"` (Bash): `World.has_shield` reads the off-hand item's `"shield": true`. Being attacked targets the attacker when you have nothing hostile targeted (`World._notice_attacker`, called on every swing, hit or miss, and in `damage`).
- The hotbar is `HotSlot` buttons (`scripts/ui/hot_slot.gd`): icon on a gem colored by kind, key in the corner, cooldown sweep, dimmed when unusable, lit for toggles. The HUD only feeds it state (`_update_hotbar`); any timer in `p.cooldowns` sweeps, including `"ranged"`. Spell and action icons are rendered by `tools/blender/spell_icons.py` into `assets/icons/spell_<id>.png` / `action_<name>.png` (`GameData.icon(name)`); a new spell needs a builder there, or it shows its initials.
- Quests (`data/quests.json`) are item hand-ins to a `giver`; `start_keyword` is optional, and `"next"` makes a quest line: completing a step accepts the next, whose giver may be anyone (A Proper Pack: Tovin -> Warden Holt -> Tovin). Each step's `first_reward_item` is usually what the next step wants.
- Ranged attacks: an item with `"slot": "range"` has `skill` (archery/throwing), `range`, `delay` and optional `ammo` (an `ammo_type` consumed from the pack, which adds its `dmg` and names the `projectile` drawn). `World.request_ranged` checks range, `in_sight` (a ray on `Layers.WORLD`) and the reload, and a miss still adds hate so it pulls. A hit on a mob has `config.ammo_recover_chance` (15%) to lodge the ammo in it (`"lodged_ammo"` meta), and `_kill_mob` puts it on the corpse as one stack. The flight is only a picture: `World.shot_fired` -> `Projectile.launch` on every machine that draws (`Net.broadcast_shot` online).
- Loot is EQ-style: humanoid mobs roll `gear` (mobs.json, tables in `data/loot.json`) when they spawn, show it (weapon in hand; `gear_parts` in models.json name the mesh parts shown per slot) and drop it on death. Dropped gear carries a quality in its id (`iron_dagger@fine`); always read items through `GameData.item(id)` / `item_name`, never `GameData.items[id]` directly. Quality odds rise with mob level and `"named": true`. Each tier's `"finish"` in `loot.json` (tint, metallic, roughness, rim, glow) shows on the gear itself: `look.tiers` ({slot: tier}, from `GameData.gear_tiers`) goes with the look, and `CharacterModel.set_tiers` refinishes held and worn meshes (originals are kept on each mesh, so finishes never stack). Compare with `--lineup=barbarian,barbarian,barbarian --tiers=crude,,masterwork --weapon=sword_1handed --worn=head:iron_coif`.
- Music is original, composed by `tools/audio/music.py` (numpy synthesis: harp, lute, flute, pad, bass, frame drum, bell, convolution reverb; seeded phrase-by-phrase melody over a chord progression per section) and encoded to `assets/music/<track>.ogg` by Blender's FFmpeg: `/Applications/Blender.app/Contents/MacOS/Blender -b --factory-startup -P tools/audio/music.py -- --out assets/music [--only greenmoor]`. A zone names its theme with `"music"`; the `Music` autoload loops it and crossfades on zoning (title screen: `title`). `combat` fades in over the zone theme (buses "MusicZone" / "MusicCombat" under "Music") while `Player.threatened` (server: any living mob hates you, sent with the player's state) and gives way after 4 calm seconds. Volume is `Controls.music_volume` (default 25%) on the "Music" bus, in Settings and the Esc menu; test runs are muted. `settings.json` is shared by `main.gd` and `Controls`, so always rewrite one key (`Controls._save_setting`), never the whole file.
- Physics layers are in `scripts/core/layers.gd`. Entities don't collide with each other.

## Verify changes

```sh
/Applications/Godot.app/Contents/MacOS/Godot --headless --path . --import   # parse/compile check
/Applications/Godot.app/Contents/MacOS/Godot --path . -- --autotest --shots=<dir>  # scripted playthrough + screenshots
/Applications/Godot.app/Contents/MacOS/Godot --path . -- --autotest --only=items,gear --shots=<dir>  # just some sections (see SECTIONS in scripts/dev/autotest.gd)
/Applications/Godot.app/Contents/MacOS/Godot --path . -- --lineup=gnoll,rat --shots=<dir>  # art check: models side by side, 4 angles
/Applications/Godot.app/Contents/MacOS/Godot --path . -- --lineup=barbarian,knight,gnoll --worn=head:iron_coif,chest:studded_tunic --shots=<dir>  # the same gear on several bodies
/Applications/Godot.app/Contents/MacOS/Godot --path . -- --flowtest  # title screen -> world -> zone out; one zone and one HUD at each step
```

Test only what is new or changed: run the matching autotest sections, and add new checks as a new `_t_<name>` section rather than appending to another.
